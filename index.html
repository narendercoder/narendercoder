Appointment & Removal in Indian Polity

Subject: General Awareness | Lecture: 30-Minute Polity Revision

Appointments • Tenure • Removal • Committees • Constitutional Articles

★★★ Cannot skip★★ Frequently asked★ Skip if short on time

🧭 Master Map — remember the power chain

Rule: Usually, the authority that appoints is equal to or higher than the authority that removes. But constitutional protection may shift removal to Parliament / President after a prescribed inquiry.
Mnemonic: “A-R-P”
Appointment → Removal authority → Procedure. For every office, first ask these three things.

Exam lens: SSC CGL generally asks who appoints, who removes, tenure, article and committee members. The lecture is UPSC-oriented, so only directly useful polity facts are retained here.

🔴 PART A — WHAT YOU CANNOT SKIP

1. UPSC Chairman & Members ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — The President appoints the UPSC Chairman and members. Removal is not at the President’s whim: the Supreme Court inquiry route and constitutional procedure apply.

📦 Article 316: appointment & tenure · Article 317: removal/suspension
Tenure: 6 years OR age 65, whichever is earlier.
🧠 “UPSC = President + 6/65”
U-P-S-C → President appoints; Six years / sixty-five years.

⚡ Shortcut: If the question says “UPSC member removed by Governor”, reject it instantly: Governor has no role.

⚠️ Trap: The President removes, but only through the prescribed constitutional process. Do not write “automatic removal after complaint”.

✍️ Example: Who appoints the UPSC Chairman? Ans: President of India. A member completes 6 years at age 61—he/she retires after 6 years, not at 65.

2. CAG of India ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal. Removal is like that of a Supreme Court judge: Parliament’s special-majority process followed by Presidential order.

📦 Article 148 · Tenure: 6 years OR age 65, whichever earlier · Removal: SC-judge-like procedure.
🧠 “CAG checks the Cash, President gives the Charge”
CAG = audit of public money; President appoints.

⚡ Treat “CAG removed by President alone” as false.

⚠️ Do not confuse CAG’s 65-year limit with Supreme Court judge’s 65 and High Court judge’s 62.

✍️ Example: CAG removal requires what? Ans: Parliament’s special-majority process, then President’s order.

3. Election Commission — CEC vs Election Commissioners ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — The President appoints the CEC and other Election Commissioners under Article 324. Under the 2023 law, the selection committee recommends names: Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM.

📦 CEC removal: like SC Judge — Parliament special-majority procedure + President’s order.
Other EC removal: President, on recommendation of the CEC. Term: 6 years OR age 65, whichever earlier. ✔ Corrected where lecture said the old/unclear position.
🧠 “Chief is tough; colleagues need Chief’s advice”
CEC has judge-like protection; other ECs need CEC recommendation for removal.

⚡ In a statement question, match “other EC can be removed by President on CEC recommendation”.

⚠️ The CEC is not removed by the CJI or the Cabinet. Also, the Election Commission is not a five-member body by default; its composition is as provided by law/presidential notification.

✍️ Example: Who recommends removal of an Election Commissioner? Ans: Chief Election Commissioner.

4. Judiciary: Supreme Court & High Court Judges ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — The President appoints Supreme Court judges (collegium convention) and High Court judges after constitutional consultations. Removal of either is by the President after Parliament’s special-majority address for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

📦 SC: Article 124 · retirement 65 years · removal: special majority in both Houses + President.
HC: Article 217 · retirement 62 years · transfer: President after consultation with CJI (Article 222).
🧠 “SC = 65, HC = 62”
Senior Court gets the higher retirement age. 124 → SC, 217 → HC.

⚡ Any option saying “Governor removes a High Court judge” is wrong. Governor is consulted in appointment, not removal.

⚠️ Seniority-based CJI appointment is a convention, not an express constitutional rule. Acting/additional HC judges are temporary; an additional judge cannot continue beyond the constitutional limit (normally up to 2 years).

✍️ Example: A High Court judge retires at what age? Ans: 62 years. Appointment is by President; removal follows the SC-judge-like process.

5. President & Vice-President ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — The President is elected by elected MPs of both Houses and elected MLAs of States, plus Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated members do not vote in the Presidential election. The Vice-President is elected by elected and nominated members of both Houses of Parliament.

📦 President: Articles 52, 54, 56 · removal only by impeachment for violation of Constitution, initiated in either House.
VP: Articles 63, 66, 67 · removal by Rajya Sabha effective majority + Lok Sabha agreement; no impeachment.
🧠 “President = Elected MPs + Elected MLAs”
“Vice = Parliament, Nominated included” — VP election includes nominated MPs.

⚡ First separate election from removal: President’s electoral college is not the same as the VP removal forum.

⚠️ State/UT legislators do not participate in VP election. President’s impeachment is not “ordinary majority”.

✍️ Example: Can a nominated Rajya Sabha member vote in the Presidential election? No. Can that member vote in the Vice-Presidential election? Yes.

6. PM, Council of Ministers, Governor & CM ★★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — The President appoints the PM, normally the Lok Sabha majority leader. Other Union ministers are appointed by the President on PM’s advice and hold office during the President’s pleasure. Governor is appointed by the President; CM by the Governor, normally the Assembly majority leader.

📦 PM & Union CoM: Articles 75 · Governor: Article 155 · State CM/CoM: Article 164.
Governor holds office during President’s pleasure; State ministers during Governor’s pleasure, practically on CM’s advice.
🧠 “Centre: P on PM; State: G on CM”
President appoints PM; Governor appoints CM. Ministers follow the leader’s advice.

⚡ “Pleasure” does not mean arbitrary in a majority government: loss of majority is the practical key.

⚠️ President does not appoint a PM unrelated to Lok Sabha support without the constitutional test of majority.

✍️ Example: Who appoints the Chief Minister? Governor. Who appoints the Governor? President.

7. Speaker, Deputy Speaker & Deputy Chairman ★★ GA | Polity

📌 Core concept — Lok Sabha elects its Speaker and Deputy Speaker from among its members. Rajya Sabha elects its Deputy Chairman. Removal requires an effective majority and 14 days’ notice. The Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

📦 Speaker/Deputy Speaker: Articles 93–94 · Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman: Articles 89–90 · State Legislative Council Chairman/Deputy Chairman: Articles 182–183.
🧠 “House chooses, House removes”
Each presiding officer is elected by the concerned House and removed by that House.

⚡ Removal = effective majority, not merely “simple majority”; notice = 14 days.

⚠️ VP is Chairman of Rajya Sabha but is not elected by Rajya Sabha members alone.

✍️ Example: A Lok Sabha Speaker’s removal motion needs prior notice of how long? 14 days.

8. High-yield constitutional/statutory bodies ★★ GA | Polity

Finance Commission
President every 5 years; Article 280. Recommends tax devolution; recommendations are not binding. No regular removal issue.
NCSC / NCST / NCBC
President appoints and removes. Articles 338, 338A, 338B. NCBC got constitutional status through 102nd Amendment.
Attorney General
President appoints; holds office during pleasure; no fixed tenure. Article 76. Not a regular government employee.
State PSC
Governor appoints; President removes. Article 317. Tenure 6 years or age 62, whichever earlier. ✔ Corrected from the lecture’s 65.
🧠 “280 Finance; 338 family; 76 AG; 317 PSC”
Number-map the bodies: FC–280, commissions–338 series, AG–76, PSC removal–317.

⚠️ State PSC appointment by Governor does not mean State PSC removal by Governor.

✍️ Example: Who appoints the State PSC Chairman? Governor. Who can remove him/her? President, under Article 317 procedure.

🟢 PART B — WHAT YOU CAN SKIP (if short on time)

9. State Election Commissioner ★ GA | Polity

Key points — Governor appoints. Removal is by President in the same manner and on the same grounds as a High Court judge. Article 243K. Why safe to skip: less frequent than ECI/CEC comparison.

🧠 “State election = Governor appoints, President protects.”

10. State Finance Commission ★ GA | Polity

Key points — Governor constitutes it every five years under Article 243-I. It recommends distribution of finances between State and local bodies. Why safe to skip: usually a one-line article question.

🧠 “I = Income of local bodies; 243-I.”

11. State Information Commission & CIC ★ GA | Polity

Key points — CIC and Information Commissioners: President appoints on a committee recommendation; State Information Commission: Governor appoints on a committee led by the CM. Governed by RTI Act, 2005. Why safe to skip: committee composition is detail-heavy and less common.

🧠 “RTI = PM/CM leads the recommendation.”

12. NHRC, SHRC, Lokpal & Lokayukta ★ GA | Polity

Key points — NHRC: President appoints on a six-member committee recommendation; removal by President after Supreme Court inquiry; tenure up to 3 years or age 70. SHRC: Governor appoints; removal by President after SC inquiry. Lokpal: President appoints on a selection committee including PM, Lok Sabha Speaker, LoP and CJI/nominee; removal follows prescribed inquiry. Lokayukta is under State law. Why safe to skip: committee-member counting is rarely needed for SSC compared with CEC/CAG/PSC.

🧠 “Human rights = SC check; Lokpal = PM + Speaker + Opposition + Judge.”

⚠️ Lokayukta is not governed by one uniform central constitutional procedure; State Lokayukta laws differ.

13. Solicitor General, CDS, Service Chiefs & NITI Aayog ★ GA | Polity

Key points — Solicitor General and Additional Solicitor General are statutory/non-constitutional legal offices; appointments are made through the government’s appointments process. CDS and Service Chiefs are appointed by the President on the government/Cabinet Committee on Appointments recommendation. NITI Aayog: PM is Chairperson; Vice-Chairperson and members are appointed by PM. Why safe to skip: these are mostly executive arrangements, not core constitutional articles.

🧠 “NITI = PM leads the policy team.”

14. Pro-tem Speaker & Leader of Opposition ★ GA | Polity

Key points — Pro-tem Speaker is appointed by President for Lok Sabha (Governor for State Assembly), usually the senior-most member, to administer oaths and conduct Speaker’s election; the office ends when the regular Speaker takes over. Leader of Opposition is recognised by the Speaker/Chairman; the traditional benchmark is one-tenth of House strength, though the office is governed by the 1977 Act and parliamentary practice. Why safe to skip: process-based, occasional questions only.

🧠 “Pro-tem = temporary oath + Speaker election.”

15. RBI Governor ★ GA | Economy/Polity

Key points — Formally appointed by the Central Government; RBI Act, 1934. Tenure is fixed by the government’s terms of appointment, commonly stated as up to 5 years. Why safe to skip: the question is usually factual and not a constitutional appointment/removal pattern.

🧠 “RBI = 1934 Act, Central Government.”

⏱️ 5-Minute Last-Day Revision

  1. President appoints UPSC; term 6 years or 65, whichever earlier; Article 316–317.
  2. CAG is appointed by President under Article 148; removal is like an SC judge.
  3. CEC removal is like an SC judge; other ECs need CEC recommendation for removal.
  4. CEC/EC selection committee under the 2023 law: PM, LoP Lok Sabha, Union Cabinet Minister nominated by PM.
  5. SC judge retires at 65; HC judge at 62.
  6. President appoints SC/HC judges; Parliament’s special-majority address precedes removal.
  7. President’s electoral college: elected MPs + elected MLAs of States, Delhi and Puducherry; nominated members excluded.
  8. VP election: elected + nominated members of both Houses; no State legislators.
  9. President is impeached for constitutional violation; VP is removed by RS effective majority and LS agreement.
  10. PM is appointed by President; CM by Governor; Governor by President.
  11. Speaker/Deputy Speaker are elected by the concerned House; removal needs effective majority and 14 days’ notice.
  12. Finance Commission: President every 5 years, Article 280; recommendations are not binding.
  13. State PSC: Governor appoints, President removes; 6 years/62, Article 317.
  14. Attorney General: President appoints, pleasure tenure, Article 76.
  15. Always write the removal procedure, not only the removal authority.

🎯 Expected Question Types

1. Match office with appointing authority: CAG–President, State PSC–Governor, Governor–President.
2. Compare CEC and other EC removal safeguards.
3. Choose the correct retirement age/article pair: SC–65/124, HC–62/217, UPSC–65/316.
4. Identify the correct electoral college for President versus Vice-President.
5. Statement set on effective majority, 14-day notice and removal of presiding officers.
